Before and after covered

Older adults in Ontario, and the family members who care for them, are entitled to more support than they receive. Not because they don't qualify, but because finding, understanding and applying for it is hard.

This report shows the gap as it stands, what the journey looks like with and without covered, and one worked example run through the live engine.

Figures are cited below. The worked example is computed by the same engine the app uses.

The journey, before and after

The same person trying to set up care and claim what they're owed.

Today

Five scattered steps

  1. 1
    Hear about programs by chance
    A search, a friend, or a hospital social worker. No one tells you proactively.
  2. 2
    Call each office separately
    Home care, CRA, Service Canada, CILT. Each has its own rules and phone line.
  3. 3
    Dig up your income figures
    Every income-tested program asks for numbers few people remember.
  4. 4
    Fill long forms and repeat your story
    Different forms, the same details, again and again.
  5. 5
    Miss the links between programs
    The Disability Tax Credit unlocks the Canada Disability Benefit and the RDSP. That link is invisible.
With covered

Three steps in one place

  1. 1
    Answer a few questions, or upload
    Tap, type or speak. Upload a Notice of Assessment and we read your income for you.
  2. 2
    See everything you may qualify for
    In plain language, each with its official source, and the unlock chain spelled out.
  3. 3
    Get forms prepared and the next step named
    You confirm and submit. A caregiver can follow along with your consent.
Today: many offices, many forms, and the most valuable connection left for you to discover.
With covered: one place, one set of answers, and nothing filed without you.

Read the tax credits carefully. The Disability Tax Credit and Medical Expense Tax Credit are non-refundable: they reduce tax owed. At Margaret's income she may owe little tax, so their value may come mainly from transferring the credit to a supporting family member. We show these as ceilings, not cash in hand.
